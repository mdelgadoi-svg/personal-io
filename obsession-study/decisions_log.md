| 7 | Nov 18 | Harmonious passion and obsessive passion are coded as two separate dimensions. A passage can be 1 on both | They correlate positively (.40 to .72) and both rest on loving the activity | Draft, in codebook |
| 8 | Nov 18 | Obsession vocabulary (OBS) is its own code, with the target recorded. The word alone never counts as obsessive passion | The one Passion Scale item with the word "obsessive" also loads on harmonious passion. No peer-reviewed definition of investor "obsession" was found. Keeping them apart is what makes the research question testable | Draft |
| 9 | Nov 18 | A general passion code (PAS) is added | Passages often show love of the work with no sign of which kind. Without PAS those would be forced into HP or lost | Draft |
| 10 | Nov 18 | Five PEBS expression flags are coded (positive emotion, negative emotion, quality, quantity, immersion). Body language and voice, and social interactions, are not | Body and voice are invisible in text. Social interaction cannot vary in an interview. The five are coded as the founder's self-description, which differs from the PEBS self-report format and is stated as a limit | Draft. Open question 10 for Prof. Bailey |
| 11 | Nov 18 | Markers such as guilt when not working and giving up sleep are labelled "our extension" inside OP | They fit the model's logic but are not Passion Scale items | Draft. Agreement on them to be reported separately |
| 12 | Nov 18 | Unit of analysis: one founder answer with its host question, split to about 200 words (150 to 300); answers under 40 words merged or dropped | Long enough for one thought, short enough to point at evidence, comparable to units in LLM coding studies, and it allows rates per passage and per 1,000 words | Draft |
| 13 | Nov 18 | Presence (0/1) plus intensity (1 to 7) for PAS, HP and OP. Presence only for OBS and the five flags | The brief asked for presence plus intensity. Intensity on every code would mean about 13 ratings per passage for volunteer coders. This keeps it to three | Draft. Easy to change |
| 14 | Nov 18 | A "self-relevant" gate comes first. If the passage is not about the founder's own relationship to work, all codes are 0 | Most interview passages are about product and market. The gate speeds coding and guards against the known LLM habit of over-labelling | Draft |
| 15 | Nov 18 | Host questions are kept with each passage and flagged PROMPTED when they invite passion or obsession talk. Unprompted passages will be the main result | Founders answer in the frame of the question (Kanze et al., 2018) | Draft |
| 16 | Nov 18 | Adaptation codes (ADAPT, RIGID) are written up as an optional module, not in round one | The research question needs an adaptation measure and the Excel has none, but adding codes before the first reliability check adds burden | Open |
| 17 | Nov 18 | Codebook examples are drafts. Real quotes come only from the Excel's short snippets and are marked unchecked. Where no real quote fits, an invented illustration is labelled as invented | No transcripts were available in this session | To replace after the human coding round |
| 18 | Nov 18 | Planned for Phase 3: no temperature setting. Each passage is coded two or three times with a fixed model version, and test-retest agreement is reported | Current Claude models reject the temperature parameter, so "temperature 0" from the brief cannot be used. Even where it existed it did not guarantee identical output (Rathje et al., 2024) | Open: needs Marcelo's approval |
