Opened Nov 18, 2026. This note closes the main gap in `q1_passion_vs_obsession.md`, where only the abstract was available.

**Source opened (FULL TEXT):** the authors' accepted manuscript ("in press, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin"), posted on PsyArXiv. Retrieved from https://osf.io/43vh8/download (PDF). A local copy is in `papers/` (not uploaded to GitHub).

**Published version (not opened, the SAGE site blocks scripts):** Krautter, K., Buechner, A., & Jachimowicz, J. M. (2025). Extraverts Reap Greater Social Rewards From Passion Because They Express Passion More Frequently and More Diversely. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 51(7), 1159-1172. https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672231211843 (online Nov 2023). Before quoting numbers in the final paper, check them against the published version: the preprint abstract says N total = 1,354 for the three scale studies and the published abstract says 1,373.

## What the paper says (quotes are from the accepted manuscript)

- Definition of passion used: "a strong feeling toward a personally important value/preference that sparks intentions and behaviors which express that value/preference" (quoting Jachimowicz et al., 2018, p. 9980).
- Prior research "has predominantly focused on particularly animated expressions of passion, including energetic body movements, rich body language, effusive gestures, and dynamic facial expressions (Chen et al., 2009)."
- Study 1 (dyads, N = 330): "higher levels of extraversion are related to higher levels of other-reported passion (β = 0.36, 95% CI [0.05; 0.56], p = .003), an effect that held when controlling for self-reported passion".
- Scale development: items came from participants' open-text answers about how they express passion. The final scale has "seven factors with three items each". Item stem: "Think about moments when you are passionate about your work. How do you express passion for your work?" Response scale 1 = never to 7 = always.

## The seven PEBS factors and their 21 items (Table 2 of the manuscript)

| Factor | Items | Can a transcript show it? |
|---|---|---|
| Quality of work | "I do my job particularly well." "I work with a high degree of consistency." "I put a lot of care into my work." | Only as self-description (the founder talks about care, craft, standards) |
| Positive emotions | "I am motivated to continue with my work." "I am enthusiastic about my work." "I am excited about my work." | Yes, in words |
| Body language and voice | "I show animated facial expressions." "I vary the tone and pitch of my voice." "I show energetic body movements." | No. Needs video or audio |
| Quantity of work | "I work longer hours than expected without being told so." "I invest more time in my work." "I take on extra work." | Only as self-description (hours, weekends, extra work) |
| Negative emotions | "I am stressed about my work." "I am anxious about my work." "I feel strained and exhausted." | Yes, in words |
| Social interactions | "I engage in more social interactions with my coworkers." "I reach out to others to tell them about my work." "I become more talkative in conversations about my work." | Weak. In an interview everyone is talking about their work, so this cannot tell founders apart. Talk time can be measured separately |
| Immersion | "I work with an intense focus." "I get carried away by my work." "I lose my sense of time." | Only as self-description |

The right-hand column is my reading, not the authors'.

## Other facts worth knowing

- How observers rate the factors (Study 2c, N = 399): "expressing passion through positive emotions (M = 6.16) and higher quality of work (M = 6.01) were rated as the most indicative of higher levels of passion", while "body language and voice (M = 5.26) and expressing negative emotions (M = 4.40) were rated as less representative for passion". So the channel a transcript loses (body and voice) is not the one people rate as most telling.
- Extraversion: in the validation study, "five of the seven factors of the PEBS were positively related to extraversion (.16 < rs < .29), the negative emotions factor was significantly negatively correlated (r = -.16 ...), while only the immersion factor was not significantly related to extraversion (r = .10 ...)".
- Diary study (N = 206, 1,862 daily reports): extraversion strengthened the link between daily passion and daily expression "for six out of seven factors", the exception being negative emotions. The effect came mainly from the assertiveness part of extraversion.
- Limits the authors state: the scale studies rely on self-reports of expression, which "may not necessarily align with the criteria observers actually rely on"; the diary study asked about the previous workday; many people score high on quality of work and positive emotions.
- Data, code and a codebook are at https://osf.io/t5vc2/ (not opened).

## What this means for the study

1. The PEBS is a self-report scale about behavior at work. In a transcript we can only code what the founder says about these behaviors, plus the emotions shown in the words. That is a real difference and should be stated.
2. Six of seven factors leave some trace in text. Body language and voice does not.
3. Extraversion is tied to most factors, so expressed-passion scores need a talkativeness control. Immersion and negative emotions are the least tied to extraversion.
4. Quantity of work, immersion and negative emotions are close to what people in venture capital call "obsession" (long hours, total focus, strain). Coding them separately from Vallerand's obsessive passion lets the study test whether investor "obsession" is really obsessive passion or just these expressions.
