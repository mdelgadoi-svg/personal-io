## 1. Answer

**Bottom line.** The link between OP and rigidity is strong as theory and has reasonable support in laboratory and survey studies outside entrepreneurship. I found no study that takes passion type as the predictor and measures pivoting or strategic adaptation in startups as the outcome. The closest entrepreneur study is qualitative, and several findings point the other way. The student's hypothesis is an open question, not an established result.

**(a) Theory or definition only.** The dualistic model defines OP as a controlled internalization that creates internal pressure, and states that it thwarts adaptation "by causing negative affect and rigid persistence" [Vallerand2003]. Later overviews repeat this [Vallerand2008; VallerandPaquetteRichard2022]. In this literature "rigid" means staying engaged in the activity at the cost of other life goals, for example cycling through winter [Bureau2019, a secondary description]. It does not mean refusing to change strategy inside the activity. The scale items show this: "I am willing to do anything to reach the top at work" [Peixoto2023]. For founders, one conceptual paper (no data) proposes that passion type shapes how entrepreneurs change roles after feedback [Kakarika2022].

**(b) Direct empirical support.** Outside entrepreneurship: in 5 laboratory studies OP, not HP, predicted suppression of alternative goals, including when passion was manipulated [Belanger2013a]. Six studies with over 3,000 participants tie rigid persistence mainly to OP and flexible persistence to HP [Vallerand2023]. In 400 Brazilian workers OP correlated .52 with rigid persistence, but HP also correlated .34 with it [Peixoto2023]. Under identity threat, OP predicted defensive, extreme responses among activists and devout believers [Rip2012]. In transitions, HP went with "adaptive flexibility" among 347 faculty near retirement, while OP was negatively related to self-regulation [BonnevilleRoussy2026]; among Chinese faculty adopting AI, OP was negatively related to job crafting [ZhuZhang2026]. These are self-report and mostly cross-sectional.

In entrepreneurs: 36 interviews with German founders going international link HP to "adaptive internationalization" and OP to "rigid internationalization" [StreebDrackBaum2026]. It is qualitative and inductive, and I read the abstract only.

**(c) Indirect or analogical.** Pivot research shows that identity, not only information, limits revision. Founders resist feedback when ideas are tied to the self [Grimes2018]. Feedback aimed at the core venture identity lowered willingness to pivot in 64 teams [BurnellStevenson2019, conference abstract]. "Revolutionary" founder identities produced inertia in air taxi startups [ZuzulTripsas2020]. Psychological ownership raises persistence under adversity [Zhu2018]. None of these measures passion type. Passion moves with identity centrality [Murnieks2014], which is the bridge, but the step from there to "OP blocks pivots" is an inference. Pivots are also rare and built from small accumulated changes [KirtleyOMahony2023], which makes "a pivot" hard to measure.

**(d) Findings that cut the other way.**
- Leaders' OP predicted more job creation "particularly under increasing environmental dynamism" in 105 EU projects [Patel2015]. This is the opposite of the hypothesis.
- OP athletes adjusted better in highly competitive leagues, HP athletes in less competitive ones [Amiot2006]. This fits "OP helps in the right context", but the outcome is wellbeing, not adaptation.
- In founders, OP related positively to performance through opportunity recognition [Tu2023], contributed to persisting with the venture [Fisher2018], was more evident among habitual entrepreneurs [ThorgrenWincent2015], gave mixed results for negative affect [Stroe2020], and was not uniquely related to burnout in one sample [Dory2026] though positively in another [deMol2018].
- After failure information, OP predicted better performance, driven by fear of failure [Belanger2013b].
- OP was negatively related to both grit facets in 425 students [VernerFilion2020], so "obsessed" does not simply mean "sticks with it".
- HP and OP are positively correlated (r = .40 in [Peixoto2023]) and co-occur within people [Moeller2025]. OP also rose after both good and bad firm results [Lee2024], so reverse causation is plausible.
- The work meta-analysis reports that passion streams relate to outcomes differently [Pollack2020]; I could not open its tables, or the Curran et al. meta-analysis.

**The gap, stated plainly.** No study I could find measures pivoting or adaptation to a changed context as the outcome with passion type as the predictor. Absence here means "not found with the tools available", not proven absent.

### 2.1 Theory and the dualistic model

**[Vallerand2003]** Vallerand, R. J., Blanchard, C., Mageau, G. A., Koestner, R., Ratelle, C., Léonard, M., Gagné, M., & Marsolais, J. (2003). Les passions de l'âme: On obsessive and harmonious passion. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85(4), 756-767. DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.85.4.756. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR, OA, EPMC).
- Core claim: "HP promotes healthy adaptation whereas OP thwarts it by causing negative affect and rigid persistence."
- Evidence base: "Results from four studies involving more than 900 participants from different populations supported the proposed conceptualization."
- The abstract does not describe the cycling or gambling studies. See [Bureau2019] and [deJonge2020] for secondary descriptions.

**[Vallerand2008]** Vallerand, R. J. (2008). On the psychology of passion: In search of what makes people's lives most worth living. Canadian Psychology, 49(1), 1-13. DOI 10.1037/0708-5591.49.1.1. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR, OA).
- OP "leads people to experience an uncontrollable urge to engage in the activity. It is hypothesised to predict less adaptive outcomes."

**[VallerandPaquetteRichard2022]** Vallerand, R. J., Paquette, V., & Richard, C. The Role of Passion in Psychological and Cardiovascular Responses: Extending the Field of Passion and Positive Psychology in New Directions. Frontiers in Psychology, 12 (Crossref issued 13 Jan 2022; Europe PMC lists 2021). DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.744629. FULL TEXT (FT, PMC8792226; read the theory passages only).
- "However, passion can also arouse negative emotions, lead to inflexible persistence, and interfere with achieving a balanced, successful life."

**[Bureau2019]** Bureau, A., Blom, L. C., Bolin, J. E., & Nagelkirk, P. R. (2019). Passion for Exercise: Passion's Relationship to General Fitness Indicators and Exercise Addiction. International Journal of Exercise Science, 12(5), 122-135. DOI 10.70252/zrwi2865. FULL TEXT (FT, PMC6355203). Used only as a SECONDARY description of Vallerand et al. 2003.
- "The researchers found that 30% of the cyclists continued cycling in the winter (when weather is not as conducive to cycling), and these individuals had higher levels of OP than HP."

**[deJonge2020]** de Jonge, J., Balk, Y., & Taris, T. (2020). Mental Recovery and Running-Related Injuries in Recreational Runners: The Moderating Role of Passion for Running. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 17(3), 1044. DOI 10.3390/ijerph17031044. FULL TEXT (FT, PMC7037891). SECONDARY description only.
- "cyclists with OP were still cycling in winter on icy roads, and thus engaged in risky (i.e., injury-promoting) activities"

**[Vallerand2023]** Vallerand, R. J., Chichekian, T., Verner-Filion, J., & Bélanger, J. J. (2023). The two faces of persistence: How harmonious and obsessive passion shape goal pursuit. Motivation Science, 9(3), 175-192. DOI 10.1037/mot0000303. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR for citation; S2 for abstract).
- "Flexible persistence takes origins in harmonious passion for an activity and entails pursuing activity goals with an open and broad focus that allows one to attend to other life goals."
- Rigid persistence "facilitates reaching some activity goals and outcomes, but not outcomes outside of the activity"
- "Results from six studies involving over 3,000 participants from different populations using correlational, prospective, and experimental research designs provide support for this new perspective on persistence."

**[Peixoto2023]** Peixoto, E. M., Silva, M. V., Noronha, A. P. P., Chichekian, T., & Vallerand, R. J. (2023). Psychometric properties and measurement invariance of the Rigid and Flexible Persistence Scale in a Brazilian professional sample. Psicologia: Reflexão e Crítica, 36(1). DOI 10.1186/s41155-022-00240-0. FULL TEXT (FT, PMC9807980).
- Sample: "Participants were 400 professionals, 55.8% women, aged between 18 and 68 years."
- Result: "Flexible persistence showed positive association with harmonious passion and occupational self-efficacy. Conversely, rigid persistence showed positive association with obsessive passion."
- Correlation table (scale scores): OP with rigid persistence .516; HP with rigid persistence .341; HP with flexible persistence .420; OP with flexible persistence -.042; HP with OP .401.
- Item wording: rigid "I am willing to do anything to reach the top at work"; flexible "I work hard at my work goals, but other things matter as well".
- Definition reported from the original scale paper: rigid persistence "is characterized by continuously seeking the achievement of a goal irrespective of conditions".
- Limitation: "The data collected and analyzed came from a convenience sample during the COVID-19 pandemic."

### 2.3 Goal conflict, cognitive narrowing, identity threat

**[Belanger2013a]** Bélanger, J. J., Lafrenière, M.-A. K., Vallerand, R. J., & Kruglanski, A. W. (2013). When passion makes the heart grow colder: The role of passion in alternative goal suppression. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 104(1), 126-147. DOI 10.1037/a0029679. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR, OA, EPMC).
- "Results from 5 laboratory studies supported these hypotheses."
- "Obsessive (but not harmonious) passion predicted the suppression of alternative goals and the progressive inhibition of unfamiliar goals."
- "In Study 3, the causal influence of passion on goal-shielding was supported via an experimental manipulation of passion."
- Study 5 looked at "the depletion of self-regulatory resources".

**[Belanger2013b]** Bélanger, J. J., Lafrenière, M.-A. K., Vallerand, R. J., & Kruglanski, A. W. (2013). Driven by fear: The effect of success and failure information on passionate individuals' performance. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 104(1), 180-195. DOI 10.1037/a0029585. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR, OA, EPMC).
- "Obsessive passion, characterized by a rigid and defensive mode of functioning, predicted greater performance in domains both related and unrelated to the passionate activity in response to exposure to failure information."
- "harmonious passion, characterized by a flexible, nondefensive mode of functioning, was found to be unaffected by success or failure information."
- Boundary: performance rose "only when the performance task was framed in such a way that failure would entail important negative consequences for the self".
- Note: "rigid and defensive" is the authors' characterization, not something this paper measures as an outcome. Four studies.

**[Rip2012]** Rip, B., Vallerand, R. J., & Lafrenière, M.-A. K. (2012). Passion for a Cause, Passion for a Creed: On Ideological Passion, Identity Threat, and Extremism. Journal of Personality, 80(3), 573-602. DOI 10.1111/j.1467-6494.2011.00743.x. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR, OA, EPMC).
- "Obsessive ideological passion, in contrast, was expected to engender hatred and aggressive extremism in identity-threatening circumstances partly because it is anchored in a strong, but insecure, sense of identity."
- "Results from 2 studies, conducted with nationalist activists (N = 114) and devout Muslims (N = 111), supported these hypotheses."

**[SchellenbergBailis2019]** Schellenberg, B. J. I., & Bailis, D. (2019). Betraying passion: Harmonious passion, obsessive passion, and attitudes following counter-attitudinal advocacy. PREPRINT (PsyArXiv, posted 26 Feb 2019). DOI 10.31234/osf.io/7q4ms. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR, OA).
- Sample: "Undergraduates (N = 81) who were passionate about various sport/exercise activities"
- "decreases in passion were only evident when passion involved high levels of OP and low levels of HP"
- Relevance: attitude and passion change depended on the combination of HP and OP, not on OP alone. Small sample, not peer reviewed as retrieved.

**[VernerFilion2020]** Verner-Filion, J., Schellenberg, B. J. I., Holding, A. C., & Koestner, R. (2020). Passion and grit in the pursuit of long-term personal goals in college students. Learning and Individual Differences, 83-84, 101939. DOI 10.1016/j.lindif.2020.101939. ABSTRACT ONLY (CR for citation; S2 for abstract).
- "harmonious passion was only positively associated with the facet of perseverance of effort. In contrast, obsessive passion was negatively associated with both perseverance and consistency of interests facets of grit."
- Sample: "a sample of university students (N = 425)".

### 2.4 Adaptation to change outside entrepreneurship

### 2.5 Entrepreneurs and passion type

## 4. Implications for the study design

- **Present the hypothesis as untested and two-sided.** Cite [StreebDrackBaum2026] as the closest support (qualitative) and [Patel2015] as a published result in the opposite direction (OP helped more under environmental dynamism). A pre-registered test that allows either sign will be more credible to a social psychologist than a one-directional claim.
- **Separate four things in the codebook that founder talk blends together:** (1) intensity and time investment, which HP and OP share; (2) OP proper (internal pressure, cannot stop, conflict with the rest of life, self-worth tied to the venture); (3) identity fusion with the idea or with a mission; (4) stated willingness to change course. The pivot literature points to (3) as the mechanism [Grimes2018; BurnellStevenson2019; ZuzulTripsas2020]. The word "obsessed" in a podcast may be only (1).
- **Score HP and OP as two continuous dimensions per founder, not as types.** They correlate around .40 [Peixoto2023] and co-occur [Moeller2025], and one preprint found effects only for particular combinations [SchellenbergBailis2019]. Report the HP by OP interaction and the HP and OP correlation in the coded language.
- **Mind what "rigid" means in the source theory.** In the dualistic model rigid persistence is staying in the activity at a cost to other life domains [Vallerand2023; Peixoto2023]. A pivot keeps the founder in the venture. OP could therefore predict refusing to quit rather than refusing to pivot, or even more pivoting to keep the company alive. Code pivot, persistence without change, and exit as three separate outcomes.
- **Define the outcome with the pivot literature, not with press labels.** Pivots are rare and accumulate from small changes [KirtleyOMahony2023]. Use dated changes in specific strategy elements (customer, product, business model), and measure the context change independently (a dated shock or a dynamism index), because the hypothesis is an interaction of OP with context change. The sample needs founders in both stable and changed contexts.
- **Fix the time order.** Passion shifts with performance and feedback [Lee2024; Lex2022; Murnieks2014]. Code only transcripts recorded before the context change, and control for venture performance at the time of recording, otherwise reverse causation cannot be ruled out.
- **Expect selection and audience effects.** VC-backed founders on podcasts are survivors speaking in public, and OP is more evident among habitual entrepreneurs [ThorgrenWincent2015]. State that results describe expressed passion in a selected group. Validate the LLM codes against the Passion Scale items in a small founder survey if possible.
- **Read these in full through the Haas library before writing the review:** Sirén, Patel and Wincent 2016; Curran et al. 2015; Pollack et al. 2020 (tables); Vallerand et al. 2003; Vallerand et al. 2023; Houlfort et al. 2015; Warnick et al. 2018; Streeb, Drack and Baum 2026.
